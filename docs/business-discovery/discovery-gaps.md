## Evidence to collect

**RECOMMENDATION.** Ask the owner for originals, not summaries written for the website.

1. Company registration or an equivalent identity document, with sensitive numbers stored privately and only the public facts copied into these files.
2. A product sheet per item: name, use, ingredients as labelled, size, price, status (idea, sample, on sale).
3. Photographs of product, pack, residue, and making. Note which are approved.
4. Logo files and any existing guideline, even a one-page version.
5. One recent label and, if it exists, the registration or notification reference.
6. A simple channel map: where a unit was sold in the last year, to whom, and how it travelled.
7. Any written supply arrangement for residue, with commercial terms redacted if needed.
8. The sentences the founder will and will not allow on a public page.

Do not paste customer personal data, payment credentials, or ID numbers into this folder.

## Questions for the business owner

Fifteen questions. The first ten are the highest priority. Each answer should be written back into the relevant discovery file and relabelled from UNKNOWN to REPORTED, then to VERIFIED when a document supports it.

### 1. Who started GRECE FOV, when, and where, and what experience made coffee residue the raw material?

**Why it matters.** The website is expected to carry an origin story. None exists in the project. Founder, date, and place also decide whether a “made in …” line is available.

### 2. What is the registered name, and how should the brand be written, pronounced, and described in one sentence?

**Why it matters.** Logo text, the browser title, the domain, and the first line of the homepage depend on it. Public search did not resolve the name.

### 3. For every product you currently make or sell, what is its name, format, size, and intended use? What is the jelly for?

**Why it matters.** The catalog is only a list of groups. The jelly cannot be merchandised until its use is confirmed. This answer replaces guesswork in `product-catalog.md`.

### 4. Which of those products are on sale today, which are samples, and which are ideas?

**Why it matters.** A premium site that presents prototypes as a shop will break trust at the first order. Capacity and stage also set how large the first release should be.

### 5. What kind of coffee residue do you use, who supplies it, and is there an agreement or a regular collection routine?

**Why it matters.** The sustainability page and the difference from ordinary coffee soap both depend on this. “Residue” is not yet defined.

### 6. Do you manufacture yourselves or with a partner, where, at roughly what weekly volume, and what do you check before a batch is sold?

**Why it matters.** Trust content, lead times, and wholesale promises require a true capacity. Contract manufacture also affects what a label must say if products are registered in Rwanda.

### 7. What is printed on the current labels, and which products, if any, are registered or notified with Rwanda FDA, RSB, or another authority?

**Why it matters.** Ingredient text, claims, and international shipping all hang on the label and the registration status. This project must not invent either.

### 8. What should a customer notice about a GRECE FOV product that they would not get from another coffee soap or scrub?

**Why it matters.** Differentiation is unproven. The answer might be texture, scent, source, a specific skin result, or the range itself. If the honest answer is “not yet”, the brand story should not pretend otherwise.

### 9. Who has actually bought so far, in Rwanda or elsewhere, and what did they ask or complain about?

**Why it matters.** Consumer, retail, hotel, and export audiences are still hypotheses. Real buyers show which journey to design first and which objections the site must answer.

### 10. What are the retail and wholesale prices, how does a product reach the buyer today, and which places and payment methods can you support in the next 12 months?

**Why it matters.** Premium positioning, checkout, and international pages are guesses until price, delivery, and payment are known. The software does not yet take payment, which may or may not match how you sell.

### 11. Which logo files, packs, and photographs already exist, and which of them are approved for public use?

**Why it matters.** The briefing says assets exist. They are not in the repository, so the brand audit cannot judge them. Design should start from those files.

### 12. Which claims are you willing to make in public now, and which words are off limits until you have evidence?

**Why it matters.** Premium and sustainability language can outrun the proof. An explicit deny-list prevents a later rewrite and a regulatory problem.

### 13. What should the first website release achieve: a catalog with manual order confirmation, a paid checkout, a wholesale enquiry, or a story site before commerce?

**Why it matters.** The repository is scoped to discovery and simple orders, with payments deferred unless approved. Your commercial goal may be narrower or further along. The release should follow the business, then the software.

### 14. Are any coffee producers, cafés, retailers, or other partners already involved, and may they be named?

**Why it matters.** Named partners are the credible version of a circular story. Invented partnerships would be worse than none.

### 15. Do you measure anything about residue collected, units made, or product lost, even in a notebook?

**Why it matters.** A single honest number is more useful than a sustainability manifesto. If nothing is measured, the site should say what you do and stop there.

## Recommended next steps

1. Hold a working session using the fifteen questions. Capture answers in the owner’s words.
2. Collect the evidence pack listed above and store originals outside the public website tree.
3. Update `company-profile.md`, `product-catalog.md`, `sustainability-and-production.md`, and `brand-audit.md` from those answers. Change labels from UNKNOWN to REPORTED, and to VERIFIED only when a document or artefact supports the point.
4. Decide the first audience and the first commercial job of the site (question 13) before wireframes.
5. Only then explore visual direction, using the real pack and logo rather than a blank premium mood.
6. Do not start website design or application code as the next step. The catalog, the residue definition, the fulfilment boundary, and the asset folder are still open.
