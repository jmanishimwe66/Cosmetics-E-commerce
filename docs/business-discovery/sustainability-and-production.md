## What environmental problem the process might address

**HYPOTHESIS, not a company result.** If the input is spent coffee grounds that would otherwise be landfilled or incinerated, using them as a cosmetic ingredient can keep a food by-product in use and can replace a synthetic scrub particle. A 2023 review describes landfill and incineration of spent coffee grounds as polluting disposal routes and discusses higher-value uses. Source: [Reviews in Environmental Science and Bio/Technology, 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11157-023-09669-w), accessed 26 October 2026.

That mechanism is a category-level possibility. It becomes a GRECE FOV outcome only if the business can show:

1. The input is a residue, not a primary coffee purchase.
2. The residue had a plausible disposal path before this use.
3. A quantity was collected and converted over a stated period.
4. The cosmetic process does not create a larger uncounted burden, such as unstable product that is discarded, or packaging that undermines the story.

None of those four points is evidenced today.

Rwanda produces and exports a large volume of coffee (see `customers-and-market.md` for NAEB figures). Processing coffee creates by-products at farm and washing-station level as well as spent grounds at the cup. Which of those streams GRECE FOV uses is **UNKNOWN**. The national export statistic is not a measure of this company’s impact.

## How the website should speak about this, for now

**RECOMMENDATION.**

- Describe only the steps the business can show, in order, with the source named.
- Prefer quantities and dates over adjectives.
- Say what is not yet measured.
- Keep certification marks off the site until the certificate is in hand.
- Separate the product story (what the residue does in the formula) from the environmental story (what waste path changes).
- Review every sustainability sentence against the label and against any registration wording so the site does not outrun the product.
