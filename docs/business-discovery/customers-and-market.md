## 1. Individual consumers

**Hypothesis.** A person buying for their own bathroom, or as a gift, who is willing to pay a premium price if the product feels considered and the coffee story is specific.

| Topic | Working hypothesis | What the website would need, once facts exist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivations | A sensory coffee ritual; smoother skin from a scrub; a gift with a traceable story; a preference for a product that uses a residue stream rather than a new decorative ingredient. | Plain explanation of what each product does, who it is for, and how coffee residue is used in it. |
| Likely questions | What is in it? Is the jelly a cosmetic? Will the scrub scratch? What does it smell like? How long does it last? Where does it ship, and when? Can I pay the way I usually pay? | Ingredient list, use instructions, texture and scent notes from the real product, size, price, delivery, and payment methods the business actually offers. |
| Trust requirements | A real company identity, readable labels, a way to ask a question, and claims that match the pack. | Company address once confirmed, batch and shelf-life information, contact route, and no medical claims without registration. |
| Barriers | Unclear use of the jelly; no photos; no price; uncertainty about delivery outside a city; fear of skin irritation from coffee particles or fragrance; premium price without a reason. | Comparison by use (cleanse, exfoliate, other), photos of texture and pack, and a published returns position. |
| Priority | **RECOMMENDATION.** Treat this as the primary website audience for the first release only if the business confirms it can pack and deliver single orders. | |

## 2. Retailers and resellers

**Hypothesis.** Independent shops, pharmacies, concept stores, or marketplaces that would resell finished goods.

| Topic | Working hypothesis | Website information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivations | A differentiated “made from coffee residue” story, reliable supply, a margin, and packaging that looks credible on a shelf. | Wholesale page with range, pack shots, and an enquiry form. Shelf-ready packaging facts once they exist. |
| Likely questions | Wholesale price, minimum order, lead time, shelf life on delivery, who holds registration, and whether they may sell online. | A trade sheet. Do not publish wholesale prices on the public product page unless the business wants them public. |
| Trust | Consistent batches, labelled country of origin, and a person who answers. | Named trade contact and sample policy. |
| Barriers | No proof of registration, irregular supply, packaging that does not survive retail display. | Status of each product: on sale, sample, or coming later. |

## 3. Hotels, spas, and salons

**Hypothesis.** Properties that might buy amenities, treatment products, or a small retail line. Relevance is higher if bar soap, liquid soap, or scrub can be supplied in guest or back-bar sizes. Those sizes are **UNKNOWN**.

| Topic | Working hypothesis | Website information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivations | A local or circular story for guests; a coffee ritual that fits a spa menu; amenities that match a premium room. | A short trade story and a clear “for businesses” path. Case sizes and lead times once known. |
| Likely questions | Guest-size options, fragrance level, skin-safety paperwork, invoicing, and whether branding can be co-labelled. | Amenity specifications, documents available under NDA, and whether private label exists. Private label is **UNKNOWN**. |
| Barriers | Housekeeping-scale quantities, inconsistent scent, missing safety data. | Honest capacity. A premium hotel will not wait on an undefined workshop. |

## 4. Wholesale buyers and distributors

**Hypothesis.** Buyers who move volume into several doors, including buyers outside Rwanda.

| Topic | Working hypothesis | Website information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivations | A range, not a single soap; export-ready documents; a brand that can hold a premium price. | Company profile, product specifications, and an enquiry path separate from the consumer cart. |
| Likely questions | Exclusivity, Incoterms, documents for import, registration in the destination market, and annual capacity. | These answers are **UNKNOWN**. The site should not imply export readiness. |
| Barriers | Cosmetic notification in the destination market, language of labels, and the cost of shipping water-heavy liquid soap. | Start international trade with a conversation, not a global add-to-cart button. |

## 5. International retail customers

**Hypothesis.** Individuals outside the home market who discover the brand online.

| Topic | Working hypothesis | Website information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motivations | The same as local retail buyers, plus interest in a coffee-origin story. | Country list the business can actually ship to. Duties and delivery time. |
| Barriers | Payment method, shipping cost, customs, returns across borders, and label language. | Hide or disable checkout for countries the business cannot serve. |
| Trust | A traceable company, not a catalog with no address. | Physical business identity once confirmed. |

**RECOMMENDATION.** Confirm the first real audience before designing two equal storefronts. A site that promises consumer checkout and international wholesale at the same time will overstate operations that are still unknown.

## Market context that is not a fact about GRECE FOV

Coffee is a significant Rwandan export. NAEB reported that in 2025 Rwanda exported 23,860 tonnes of green coffee and earned more than USD 148.6 million, up from 17,142 tonnes and more than USD 89.8 million in 2024. Source: [NAEB, accessed 26 October 2026](https://www.naeb.gov.rw/publications/news/rwanda-coffee-export-revenues-hit-record-150m-in-2025). The Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning’s Annual Economic Report for FY2024/25 records coffee export earnings of USD 116.2 million in that fiscal year, compared with USD 78.7 million in FY2023/24. Source: [MINECOFIN Annual Economic Report FY2024/25](https://www.minecofin.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/Minecofin/Publications/REPORTS/Chief_Economist/Macroeconomic_Policy/Annual_Economic_Reports_web/ANNUAL_ECONOMIC_REPORT_FY2024_25.pdf), accessed 26 October 2026.

This context explains why a coffee-residue story can be intelligible in Rwanda. It does not show that GRECE FOV buys from exporters, washing stations, or cafés.

Spent coffee grounds are discussed in the research literature as a large by-product of brewing, often landfilled or incinerated, and as a candidate abrasive and antioxidant source for cosmetics. A 2023 review in *Reviews in Environmental Science and Bio/Technology* describes landfill and incineration of spent grounds as environmentally damaging routes, and lists higher-value uses including antioxidants and materials. Source: [Springer, 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11157-023-09669-w). Later formulation papers discuss spent grounds as scrub particles. Those papers are not studies of GRECE FOV and do not prove a benefit for its products.
