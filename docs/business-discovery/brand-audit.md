## What the name currently communicates

**REPORTED** writing: GRECE FOV, in capitals, as two tokens.

**UNKNOWN:** pronunciation, whether FOV is an acronym, whether GRECE is meant to evoke a word in Kinyarwanda, French, English, or another language, and whether a descriptor line exists (“coffee residue personal care” or similar).

**HYPOTHESIS.** Without a descriptor, the name does not by itself tell a new visitor that the products are scrubs and soaps made from coffee residue. A future identity will probably need a short supporting line. The wording of that line should come from the confirmed product use and the confirmed residue source.

## Tone of voice

No published voice exists in the project.

**REPORTED** intentions that will affect tone once copy is written: premium, innovative, and explicit about sustainability. Premium, in voice, usually means specific and calm rather than a pile of superlatives. That is a **RECOMMENDATION** about tone, and it depends on the business accepting it.

Claims that the current evidence cannot support, and that a premium voice should avoid until proof exists:

- Zero waste, carbon neutral, chemical-free, 100 percent natural, organic.
- Medical or healing claims.
- “The only” or “the first”, unless a dated source supports it.
- Superiority over other coffee soaps or scrubs.

## Consistency across channels

**UNKNOWN.** There is no website, and no confirmed second channel, so consistency cannot be scored. The risk to plan for is the opposite: the software seed uses makeup, hair, and generic skincare names (`backend/scripts/seed.sql`). If that sample catalog were ever shown to the public, it would contradict the coffee-residue range. **RECOMMENDATION:** treat the seed catalog as private test data only.

## Territories to explore later

These are **RECOMMENDATION** territories for a workshop. They are not a chosen brand platform. More than one can be true. The right mix depends on founder motivation, the residue source, the price, and the pack.

### Premium beauty

Fits the reported positioning if the sensory experience, packaging, and price can hold it. Risks looking generic if the only cue is gold type and a leaf. Needs real product photography and a restrained claim set.

### Circular innovation

Fits a residue-to-product process if the source and the steps can be shown. This territory is about the transformation, not about a green colour. It asks for process photography, a simple diagram, and numbers when they exist. It fails if the residue story cannot be documented.

### Natural personal care

Fits soaps, scrubs, and related cosmetics if the formulas and labels support a natural-leaning story. “Natural” is a regulated and commercially crowded word. Use it only in the sense the label and the local authority allow. Kandaka Naturals already occupies a clear natural-soap position in Kigali (see `customers-and-market.md`). Overlap is a positioning question, not a reason to copy.

### Contemporary entrepreneurship from the company’s actual place

Relevant if the founders want the brand to carry where it is made and who makes it. The company’s location and founders are **UNKNOWN**, so this territory is not available to design yet. Rwanda is a reported market ambition, not a confirmed place of origin. Do not build an “African brand” story ahead of the people and the place.

## What not to decide yet

- Primary and secondary colours.
- Wordmark versus symbol.
- Serif or sans, or any type pairing.
- Photography direction beyond a working brief: photograph the real product, the residue, and the making, in the real place.
- A sustainability badge or icon set.

**RECOMMENDATION.** The first brand workshop should start with the asset folder the owner can provide, the products on the table, and the answers in `discovery-gaps.md`. Visual exploration comes after that, not before.
